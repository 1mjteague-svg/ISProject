🌊 Surf Music

Reverb, Tremolo & the Sound of the Ocean

Surf music emerged in Southern California in the early 1960s, defined by wet reverb-drenched guitar tones, tremolo picking, and minor key melodies that evoke crashing waves and open highways. Pioneered by Dick Dale and carried forward by bands like The Ventures and The Beach Boys, the genre laid the groundwork for garage rock, punk, and alternative music that followed. Today, artists like Allah-Las, Mild High Club, and The Garden carry the torch into the 21st century.

Essential Surf Bands

  1. Dick Dale & His Del-Tones — The King of Surf Guitar
    • Known for: Misirlou, Let's Go Trippin'
    • Pioneered high-speed alternate picking and reverb-heavy tone
  2. The Ventures — Walk Don't Run
    • Known for: Walk Don't Run, Pipeline, Hawaii Five-O
    • Instrumental surf at its most iconic
  3. The Beach Boys — Surf with Vocals
    • Known for: Surfin' USA, Fun Fun Fun, Good Vibrations
    • Bridged surf instrumentals and pop harmony
  4. Allah-Las — Modern Surf Revival
    • Known for: Catamaran, Tell Me (What's On Your Mind)
    • Lo-fi, dreamy, garage-influenced surf from LA
  5. Mild High Club — Psychedelic Surf
    • Known for: Windowpane, Tesselation
    • Jazzy, hazy, and deeply influenced by 60s surf and psych

Dick Dale

Often called the "King of Surf Guitar," Dale developed the reverb-heavy, lightning-fast tremolo picking style that defined the genre. His use of the Fender Showman amp and heavy strings created a sound no one had heard before.

Allah-Las

A Los Angeles band that revived classic 60s surf and garage sounds with a lo-fi, hazy aesthetic. Their self-titled debut is one of the best surf revival albums ever recorded.

Mild High Club

Blending surf, jazz, and psychedelic rock into something uniquely dreamy. Alex Brettin writes music that feels like it exists in a parallel decade somewhere between 1967 and now.
